About this tool - please read first (what it is, how it works, and important disclaimers)

What it is. A free, private tool to explore divorce settlement scenarios. It helps you see, in rough numbers, what each spouse might walk away with under different assumptions, and how trade-offs play out - keeping the home, taking more cash now versus retirement, or securing a lump sum up front. Think of it as a way to make sense of the equitable-distribution schedules and support figures your professionals prepare, and to work through your own goals before meetings.

How it works, and how the pieces connect. You enter your own assets, debts, the home, a business, and alimony inputs - every field is editable.
• Marital assets are split between you (50/50 by default; change the "% you" on any item).
• The Your totals / Spouse's totals tabs show what each of you ends up with; Division counts only what gets split, Total wealth also adds each side's separate property.
• The Home section computes equity, then either the buyout you would pay to keep it, or the tax and selling costs if you sell.
• Alimony uses a formula (for example, Florida: the lesser of your need or 35% of the net-income difference, paid over a set duration) - edit the %, income figures, and duration for your own state.
• Low / Likely / High let you test ranges on contested items; the Deal Builder suggests the best mix of assets to actually take for a goal (cash now, security, or deferring tax).
• Save current names a scenario so you can compare different versions side by side.

What "saving" means. Everything you type - including saved scenarios - stays in your own browser on your device. Nothing is uploaded, sent, or stored on any server. "Save current" keeps a named version in that browser; "Download backup" saves a file you control and can re-import on any device; "Clear / reset," or clearing your browser data, erases everything. Best practice: download a backup after any important changes so you always have a copy you trust.

Please work with a professional - this does not replace one. This is an educational planning tool, not legal, tax, or financial advice, and not a substitute for an attorney, a forensic or financial professional, or an official state calculation. Divorce laws, alimony types and caps, child-support guidelines, imputed income, and tax treatment vary a great deal by state and change over time; the examples built in (like the 35% alimony figure or the Florida durational caps) reflect one state's rules and may not apply to yours. The results are estimates based only on what you enter and on simplifying assumptions - they can be incomplete, out of date, or wrong, and the tool may contain errors. Verify every figure with your own attorney and financial professionals. Use this only to understand scenarios and prepare better questions - never to make firm decisions, negotiate, or file anything.

ⓘ How funds differ (for settlement)

Cash / taxable: available soon; taxed only on gains.

IRA: moves tax-free, but withdrawal before 59½ usually adds 10%.
Illiquid / offset: not cash - received through other assets.
