About this tool - please read first (what it is, how it works, and important disclaimers)

What it is. A free, private tool to explore divorce settlement scenarios. It helps you see, in rough numbers, what each spouse might walk away with under different assumptions, and how trade-offs play out - keeping the home, taking more cash now versus retirement, or securing a lump sum up front. Think of it as a way to make sense of the equitable-distribution schedules and support figures your professionals prepare, and to work through your own goals before meetings.

How it works

  1. Agree to the terms at the bottom of this section to unlock the calculator.
  2. Open a section (Accounts, House, Debts...) and tap a suggestion to add an item, or add your own.
  3. Type in the amounts. Marital items are split 50/50 by default; change the "% you" on any item.
  4. Enter Low, Likely, and High amounts. Likely is your best estimate. If you're unsure of a value, or you and your spouse disagree, also fill in Low and High; otherwise leave them blank and they match Likely. The Assumption buttons switch every item between the three; clicking a box tries it for one item, and Lock keeps that item at its amount no matter which Assumption button is on.
  5. Add your arguments (optional): for any item, explain why the Low, Likely, and High amounts apply, what the other side argues, and your response. Set your goals under Basic info. All of it prints with your summary.
  6. Use your own AI (optional): click Use your AI at the top right. Paste what it copies into ChatGPT, Claude or another AI, attach a new document, and click the link it returns. You approve every change before it applies.
  7. Read the results panel: what each of you walks away with, how much is cash versus retirement or tied up, and the Deal Builder's suggested mix of assets.
  8. Save and compare versions in the Scenarios section. Use the File menu at the top right to download a backup or print a summary for your attorney.

Division only counts what gets split. Total wealth also adds each person's separate property.

Tips for a complete picture

  • Include everything you and your spouse own, both marital and separate (non-marital) property, so you can see what you will actually have when the divorce is final. Mark separate items with the item-type dropdown, then switch to Total wealth to include them.
  • Include every debt: mortgages, car loans, credit cards, personal loans, and taxes owed.
  • Use one date for all values, such as your separation or filing date, and keep the statement behind each number.
  • A dollar is not always a dollar. Retirement money may be taxed or penalized when withdrawn, and a home is not cash until it is sold or refinanced. Check "money by access" in the results.
  • Add a note to any item explaining where the number came from.
  • Support rules vary by state. For alimony and child support, use your state's official guideline or calculator, or the figures from your attorney.

What "saving" means. Everything you type - including saved scenarios - stays in your own browser on your device. Nothing is uploaded, sent, or stored on any server. "Save current as a scenario" (in the Scenarios section) keeps a named version in that browser; File ▾ at the top right has "Download backup," which saves a file you control and can re-import on any device, and "Clear / reset," which (like clearing your browser data) erases everything. Best practice: download a backup after any important changes so you always have a copy you trust.
